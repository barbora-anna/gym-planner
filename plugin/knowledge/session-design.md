# Session design: what goes in, in what order

## Choosing exercises

- Start from the lifter's split and goal (profile), and from what they did last time in a
  session of the same kind: continuity is what makes progression measurable.
- Before drafting, ask whether they want to stick with their exercises or try something new
  for a muscle group. If new, offer 2–3 options from `gym find --muscle <m>`, with your pick
  and a one-line reason each.
- Prefer exercises they have equipment for and like. Respect dislikes and anything the
  health notes rule out.
- Cover the session's muscles with a mix: a heavy compound or two, then accessories. Size the
  number of exercises to the time they have and the week's sets.
- The exercise identity must be an exact catalog name (`gym find`). If the right name is
  unclear, ask.

## Order

- Heavy and skill-demanding compounds first (squat, deadlift, bench, hip thrust, overhead
  press), while the lifter is fresh. Isolation and machines later. Exercises done earlier in
  a session get more reps than the same exercises done later (Simão et al. 2012). Strength
  gains are largest for exercises done first, and order doesn't change hypertrophy (Nunes
  2021).
- One near-max effort per session at most, on the lift trained first. No study tested a
  per-session cap. The reason: later exercises get fewer reps (Simão 2012), and a computed
  RIR assumes a fresh set, so it can't see that fatigue. Plan later heavy lifts with a little
  more RIR.
- Order changes warm-ups: a second compound for already-worked muscles needs a shorter ramp
  (see `loads.md`).

## Splits

- Split and full-body routines give the same results at matched volume (Ramos-Campo 2024),
  and training frequency doesn't change hypertrophy at matched volume (Schoenfeld 2019). So
  pick the split that fits the lifter's days and gets the week's sets done:
  - 2–3 days a week: full body each session.
  - 4 days: upper / lower, or two full-body variants.
  - 5–6 days: push / pull / legs, or upper / lower plus specialisation.

  (Scheduling suggestions, not training claims.)
- Strength rises with frequency even at matched volume (Pelland 2026), so the lifts the
  lifter wants stronger benefit from being trained more often. Doing that practice at
  submaximal loads is a convention.

## Equipment reality

- Barbells weigh what the lifter's bars weigh (profile): never plan below the bar.
- Fixed-weight bars and dumbbells come in steps: plan loads that exist.
- Machines differ between gyms: stack steps, the sled, pulley ratios. Note what the lifter
  told you in the profile and plan in those units.
