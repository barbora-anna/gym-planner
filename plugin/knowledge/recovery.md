# Recovery: everything outside the plan

## Other activity

- Runs, hikes, rides and sports are **recovery context only**: never plan or analyse them.
- If the lifter uses Strava, glance at the last ~3 days of non-strength activity before
  planning (a practical window, not from a study). A long run or hike the day before a leg session is a reason for a little more
  RIR, fewer sets, or swapping the session order in the week. Ask how their legs feel rather
  than guessing.
- Endurance work interferes with strength and hypertrophy most when it's leg-dominant and
  frequent or long (Wilson et al. 2012). Keep hard runs and heavy leg days apart when the
  lifter has the choice.

## Readiness

- Ask briefly before a session when it matters: sleep, soreness, stress, time available.
- The first working set tells you the day. Helms 2018 adjusted the next set's load when RPE
  fell outside a 2-point range (trained men, n = 10 vs 11). The thresholds below follow
  that; their exact values weren't compared in any trial:
  - 2+ reps further from failure than planned → up for the remaining sets.
  - 2+ reps closer, or reps missed → down.
  - Within ±1 → keep the load. Reported RIR is that noisy (`loads.md`).
  - Compute both loads in advance: `gym calc load <reps> --rir <target> --from
    <W>x<reps>@<target±2>`. Write the actual kg in the plan's notes so the page shows them.
  - Untrained or novice lifters: no mid-session increase. Note it for next time instead
    (Helms 2016, an expert recommendation, not a trial).
- Soreness alone isn't a reason to skip: training 2 and 4 days after a muscle-damaging bout
  didn't worsen the damage, though force was lower (Nosaka 2002). Pain is different.

## Pain and injury

- Don't diagnose. Record what the lifter tells you in the profile's health section (where,
  since when, what aggravates it).
- Plan around it: swap the aggravating exercise for one that doesn't hurt, reduce range or
  load, and keep training everything else.
- Suggest a physiotherapist or doctor for sharp pain, pain that changes how they move,
  numbness, or anything that lasts beyond a couple of weeks.

## Deloads and layoffs

- No fixed deload schedule: a scheduled 1-week deload gave equal muscle growth and less
  strength gain than training straight through (Coleman 2024). Reduce volume (fewer sets,
  more RIR) when performance drops across several sessions, or when life stress is high, or
  the lifter asks. That trigger is a convention.
- After a break, see `progression.md` → "When things change".
